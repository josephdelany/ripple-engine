## Narrative

On July 3, 1988, the USS Vincennes and USS Elmer Montgomery, operating in international waters of the Persian Gulf near the Strait of Hormuz, engaged Iranian small boats that had fired on a Vincennes helicopter conducting a patrol [S3]. During that engagement, at approximately 1054 local time, the Vincennes fired on and destroyed Iran Air Flight 655, an Airbus A300 en route from Bandar Abbas to Dubai, killing all 290 aboard [S1][S2]. Reagan's same-day statement said the Vincennes acted in "proper defensive action," believing the airliner "headed directly" for the ship amid the ongoing small-boat engagement [S1]. Iran later brought suit at the ICJ, framing the same event as the destruction of a civilian airliner by a U.S. warship [S2]. No source retrieved this session states a barrel, tanker, or infrastructure figure connected to this event — the incident involved no attack on oil production, transit, or storage. What was known same-day: a civilian Iranian airliner had been shot down by a U.S. Navy ship during active combat with Iranian forces, with the U.S. framing it as a defensive mistake [S1][S3]. What was not known: Iran's legal response (suit filed nearly 11 months later) or the eventual 1996 negotiated settlement [S2].
