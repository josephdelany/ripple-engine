## Narrative

By October 1997, per a contemporaneous BIS account, "the main cause of the propagation of the Asian currency crisis to South Korea" was the deteriorating position of local Korean banks following the collapse of heavily indebted conglomerates [S1]. "The package of liberalisation measures announced in November failed to restore market confidence and, in December, the Korean Government turned to the IMF for a rescue programme," eventually "a record-breaking support package of $57 billion" [S1]. By the Federal Reserve's December 16, 1997 policy meeting, Chairman Greenspan and colleagues discussed Korea as one of "the three economies in Asia that already are in the hands of the International Monetary Fund" [S2], and Governor McTeer observed that IMF-mandated austerity meant "it is hard to save your way to prosperity in an environment of collapsing aggregate demand" [S2] — the demand-contraction logic linking Korea's crisis to weaker oil demand. No source retrieved this session dates a specific "Korea requests IMF bailout" announcement to November 21, 1997; the best-sourced contemporaneous account instead places Korea's formal "turn to the IMF" in December, describing November only as a failed liberalization package [S1]. This dossier flags, rather than silently resolves, that discrepancy against the existing November 21 event_date.
