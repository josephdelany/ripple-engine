## Narrative

On August 2, 1990, Iraqi forces invaded and occupied Kuwait [S2], and a force reported at one hundred thousand Iraqi troops overran the country within hours [S3]. That same day, President George H.W. Bush stated: "The United States strongly condemns the Iraqi military invasion of Kuwait. We call for the immediate and unconditional withdrawal of all Iraqi forces" [S1]. Bush announced he had "signed an Executive order early this morning freezing Iraqi assets in this country and prohibiting transactions with Iraq" and "an Executive order freezing Kuwaiti assets," explaining the latter was "to ensure that those assets are not interfered with by the illegitimate authority that is now occupying Kuwait" [S1]. Bush linked the crisis to oil dependence, noting the US was "dependent for close to 50 percent of our energy requirements on the Middle East" [S1], though he declined to discuss military options: "I'm not contemplating such action, and I again would not discuss it if I were" [S1]. The UN Security Council adopted Resolution 660 the same day, condemning the invasion and demanding Iraq's "immediate and unconditional withdrawal" to its pre-invasion positions [S2]. Despite awareness of Iraqi threats against Kuwait through July, the United States "did not anticipate the Iraqi military incursion" [S3].

## Not known at the time

On August 2, 1990, whether the international response would extend to military action was explicitly unresolved: Bush stated "I'm not contemplating such action, and I again would not discuss it if I were" [S1]. The scale of subsequent UN action was not yet known — S2 records that mandatory arms and economic sanctions followed only "a few days later," and that the Security Council went on to adopt 12 resolutions between August 2 and November 29, 1990, "culminating in resolution 678 (1990)," which authorized "all necessary means" against Iraq if it did not comply by January 15, 1991 — none of which had occurred as of August 2. The eventual coalition military campaign (Operation Desert Storm, beginning January 17, 1991, a separate event in this corpus) had not been decided or announced. No source retrieved this session states a specific pre-invasion Kuwaiti crude production or export figure in barrels per day; that stake is not quantified here rather than estimated.
