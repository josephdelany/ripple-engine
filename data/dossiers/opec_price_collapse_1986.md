## Narrative

By 1985 Saudi Arabia's attempt to defend the oil price as OPEC's "swing producer" — cutting its own output to absorb others' overproduction — had driven its production from about 10 million b/d in 1980 to about 3.4 million b/d in 1985, a roughly three-quarters reduction [S1], matching Hamilton's independent estimate that the Saudis "voluntarily shut down 3/4 of its production between 1981 and 1985" [S2]. This dossier found **two distinct, source-attested moments** in the abandonment of that policy and could not establish which the market would have treated as the decisive dated announcement. First, in September 1985 at the Oxford Energy Seminar, Oil Minister Yamani said Saudi Arabia was "being forced to abandon its official price policy and essentially join the netback bandwagon", telling producers "since we can't convince you to manage the market by cutting production back, we are going to join you" [S1]. Second, OPEC's 71st Conference met in Vienna on 10-11 December 1985 [S3], which secondary sources associate with a "secure and defend a fair share" decision and with Yamani saying Saudi Arabia would "raise production by 50%" [S4]. Neither the Conference communique nor an exact day within that window could be retrieved this session, so the choice between the two anchors is left to Joe rather than manufactured here. What was physically at stake, per S1's figures: several million barrels a day of withheld Saudi capacity returning to the market over the following months.

## Class

Proposed class: `opec_decision`, as currently coded. Codebook clause: "`opec_decision` | OPEC/OPEC+ production decision or collapse of talks." Whichever specific date is chosen, the underlying fact pattern — OPEC's abandonment of production restraint in favor of a market-share strategy — is squarely an OPEC production decision, a clean fit. The complication is not the class but the date, addressed above.

## Not known at the time

Neither S1 nor S2 nor S4 establishes what oil-market participants expected the day before either candidate date. What was not known at the time, per S1's own later (2015) framing, is how far prices would fall: S1 states "prices proceeded to crash into single digits" as a consequence the author frames as not fully anticipated even by industry insiders present at the September 1985 seminar ("Yamani's simple, straightforward explanation was so clear, direct, and unvarnished that it was not immediately understood by all, or at least its consequences were not" — S1). This dossier does not quote a specific number for the eventual price low because that would be a price-reaction fact, not a knowable-at-the-time fact, and is in any case outside this event's scope.
