## Narrative

On December 10, 1996, Iraq's first oil exports under UN Security Council Resolution 986 began, after Baghdad "delayed implementation... for a year and a half" following the resolution's April 1995 adoption [S2]. A UN Security Council press release states the Iraq-UN memorandum of understanding "paved the way for Iraqi oil exports, which began on 10 December 1996" [S1]. Resolution 986 had authorized Iraq to sell "up to $1 billion of oil every 90 days for a total of $2 billion during a 180-day period" to fund humanitarian imports [S2] — a financial cap set and publicly known since April 1995, well before the December 1996 start. The U.S. Government Accountability Office later confirmed "Phase I of the Oil for Food program began" on "Dec. 10, 1996" [S3]. This was the first return of Iraqi crude to world markets since the August 1990 embargo, under a capped, UN-supervised arrangement rather than a full lifting of sanctions. In the subsequent authorized period beginning June 1997, "24 liftings -- totalling 28.7 million barrels, with an estimated value of $456.2 million" were completed in the first 90 days [S4]; no source retrieved this session gives Phase I's own (December 1996–June 1997) throughput.

## Knowable at

1996-12-10, day precision. Reason: three independent sources (S1, S2, S3) each state that exports "began" on this date. This is established from documentary/institutional records retrieved this session, not from a same-day wire report — no press account of December 10, 1996 itself was retrieved, so the exact hour is not knowable from these sources. The financial scale of the program (up to $2 billion per 180 days) had been public since Resolution 986's April 1995 adoption [S2], and the Iraq Sanctions Committee was visibly working through implementation procedures by late May 1996, per a UN press release retrieved but not counted as a numbered source here (press.un.org/en/1996/19960528.sc6224.html, 28 May 1996), consistent with a long, non-sudden build-up rather than a same-day surprise.

## Not known at the time

On December 10, 1996, the actual volume Iraq would export during Phase I (December 1996–June 1997) was not established by any source retrieved this session — only later-period figures (Phase II's first 90 days: 28.7 million barrels [S4]) were found. Whether Iraq would continue to comply with the program was unknown at the start: Clinton's February 1998 letter states that "since December 1996, the Iraqi regime" continued to "obstruct and delay the relief plan" [S2], a pattern only observable in the ensuing months and years, not on the start date itself. The program's eventual 2003 termination, tied to the U.S.-led invasion, was not knowable in 1996.
