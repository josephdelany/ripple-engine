## Narrative

On May 6, 1995, President Clinton signed Executive Order 12959, "Prohibiting Certain Transactions With Respect to Iran," acting "in addition to those set forth in Executive Order No. 12957 of March 15, 1995" to address "the unusual and extraordinary threat" the order found Iran posed [S1]. It barred U.S. persons from importing Iranian-origin goods, exporting goods, technology or services to Iran or Iranian-government-controlled entities, and making "any new investment by a United States person in Iran or in property...owned or controlled by the Government of Iran" [S1][S2][S3] — extending to a total embargo the narrower investment ban EO 12957 had begun two months earlier. Section 3 directed Treasury to collect "reports by United States persons on oil transactions engaged in by their foreign affiliates with Iran," the order's only explicit reference to oil [S1]. The order took effect 12:01 a.m. EDT May 7, 1995, with existing trade contracts wound down by June 6 [S1]. On its face, Section 1's prohibitions bind only United States persons and U.S.-origin transactions; the text does not purport to restrict Iran's oil sales to non-U.S. buyers [S1]. What was known May 6, 1995: the full order text, its effective date, and that it was signed that day at the White House [S1].

Oil mechanism (codebook inclusion criterion 3), stated from retrieved evidence: EO 12959 bars "any new investment by a United States person in Iran or in property...owned or controlled by the Government of Iran" [S1], extending EO 12957's narrower ban on U.S. investment in Iranian petroleum-resource development [S1] to all Iranian investment — cutting off U.S. capital, technology and oilfield-services participation that could otherwise have expanded or sustained Iranian oil production and export capacity.
