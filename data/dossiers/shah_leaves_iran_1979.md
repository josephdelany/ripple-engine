## Narrative

On January 17, 1979, President Carter confirmed publicly that "the Shah has left Iran," characterizing it — based on what the Iranian government was telling the U.S. at that point — as a trip taken "for a vacation," with its duration undetermined [S1]. The same day, Carter stated the United States had "no intention... to interfere in the internal affairs of Iran" and disclaimed any intention of permitting other nations to do so [S1]. On the oil side, a National Security Council staff memo dated the day before, January 16, reported that management of Iranian oil production by foreign technicians "seems to be finished" and that oil-well investment programs had "been suspended for three months," while noting Saudi Arabia was producing at "maximum levels (10.3 mmbd)" and weighing whether to lift its own 8.5 mmbd 1979 production ceiling [S2]. Carter separately quantified the direct U.S. stake as modest — "about 5 percent of our oil supplies" came from Iran in recent months [S1] — locating the immediate risk less in direct U.S. supply than in the broader world market the Saudi-ceiling discussion was meant to address [S2]. What was known on January 17 was that the Shah had left and that his return date was unknown; what was not yet known, and is not asserted here, is that the departure would become permanent exile, or what government would follow.

## Class

Proposed class as currently coded: `conflict_escalation`. Codebook clause: "War, invasion, major military escalation involving a producer/transit state." Neither retrieved source describes the Shah's departure as a military act — it is a political/personal departure ("for a vacation," per the information available to Carter on January 17 [S1]) occurring in the context of a broader revolutionary crisis. Strictly read, this is not a war, invasion, or military escalation. It is defensible only loosely, as one step in an unfolding domestic political-conflict process that both sources treat as consequential to producer-state stability [S1][S2] — but it is a weaker fit than, for example, the November 1979 hostage-taking (a direct state-to-state act, see `iran_hostage_crisis_1979`). No other class in the closed set fits better: it is not a chokepoint disruption, an OPEC decision, a sanctions action, an infrastructure attack, or a demand shock. This is flagged as a defensible-but-imperfect fit rather than an outright miscoding, unlike the strike event's `infrastructure_attack` label.

## Not known at the time

On January 17, Carter's own characterization was that the Shah's absence was "for a vacation" with an undetermined return date [S1] — not yet framed, publicly, as a permanent exile or the end of the monarchy. The eventual outcome (permanent exile, the February 1979 revolution, Khomeini's assumption of power) was not known on January 16–17 and is not asserted here as having been known then.
