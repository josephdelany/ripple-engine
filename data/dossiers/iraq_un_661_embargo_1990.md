## Narrative

On August 6, 1990, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 661, four days after Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. The resolution directed states to prevent imports of "all commodities and products originating in Iraq or Kuwait," block export or trans-shipment of goods to either country, and prohibit making "funds or any other financial or economic resources" available to Iraq's government, with exceptions only for medical supplies and humanitarian foodstuffs [S2]. Three days later, President Bush's Executive Order 12724 cited the resolution by name and number — "United Nations Security Council Resolution No. 661 of August 6, 1990" — and implemented parallel U.S. prohibitions on importing "any goods or services of Iraqi origin" and on dealing in Iraqi-origin property exported "after August 6, 1990" [S1]. A U.S. Defense Department reference page separately records the resolution's adoption date and vote, 13 in favor, none against, two abstentions [S3]. No source retrieved this session states a barrel-per-day figure for the volume of Iraqi or Kuwaiti crude removed from world markets by the embargo; that figure, if it exists in the corpus description, was not independently verified this session and is not repeated here as sourced.

## Not known at the time

The embargo's eventual duration (it remained in force, later modified by Resolution 986's oil-for-food exception in 1995, per this corpus's own `iraq_un_986_ofp_1995` event) was not knowable on August 6, 1990. Whether Iraq would withdraw from Kuwait under sanctions pressure alone, obviating the need for military action, was undetermined — the Gulf War air campaign did not begin until January 17, 1991, more than five months later. The realized scale of Iraqi and Kuwaiti export loss in barrels/day is not established by any source retrieved this session and so cannot be reported as known-then or known-now from this dossier's evidence.
