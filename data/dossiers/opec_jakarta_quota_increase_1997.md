## Narrative

OPEC's Conference of oil ministers met in Jakarta from 26 November to 1 December 1997 [S2]. Saudi Arabia — which had already begun accepting liftings above its own existing quota during 1997 without publicly advertising the policy change [S2] — persuaded the other members to raise production quotas by 10 percent, reaching agreement "apparently without great difficulties" [S1]. Mabro's contemporaneous account frames one Saudi objective as seeking to "legalise the actual production policy" already under way [S2]. The decision landed just as the Asian financial crisis began depressing Asian oil demand; a commentator later quipped that ministers "could not see the Asian economic crisis by looking down from their hotel windows in Jakarta" [S1]. Prices, which had held near $18 a barrel, began a sustained slide within days of the conference closing, a chronological link Mabro treats as probably causal [S2]. This dossier could not independently confirm the specific absolute quota levels (elsewhere cited as 25.0 to 27.5 million b/d) from any source retrieved this session — only the 10 percent figure is source-supported [S1]. What was physically at risk was several million barrels a day of additional OPEC supply reaching an already weakening market [S1][S2]. (196 words)

## Not known at the time

Per [S1], the severity of the incoming Asian financial crisis's demand impact was not apparent to ministers at Jakarta ("could not see the Asian economic crisis by looking down from their hotel windows"). Per [S2], Saudi Arabia's proposal did not account for the fact that several members (Venezuela, Nigeria, Qatar named) were already producing above even the *new*, higher quotas — meaning the effective supply increase would exceed the nominal 10 percent, a fact [S2] frames as understood only in hindsight. The specific absolute "before" and "after" barrel levels are not established by any source retrieved this session (see Sources note); this dossier flags that as an unresolved gap, not a fact settled in either direction.
