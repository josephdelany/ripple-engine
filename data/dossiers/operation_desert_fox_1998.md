## Narrative

On December 16, 1998, Clinton announced from the Oval Office: "Earlier today I ordered America's Armed Forces to strike military and security targets in Iraq" [S1], stating the mission was "to attack Iraq's nuclear, chemical, and biological programs and its military capacity to threaten its neighbors" [S1], following what he called Iraq's failure of "its final chance" after "Chairman Butler's report" the previous night found UNSCOM "not able to conduct the work mandated to it" [S1]. The same evening the UN Security Council convened its 3955th meeting; Iraq's representative said "the United States had once again flouted international law," the U.S. representative countered that "the resort to military action was undertaken only when it was evident that diplomacy had been exhausted," and Russia's representative argued "no one could act independently on behalf of the United Nations" [S2]. Clinton's address noted the sanctions regime still "allows Iraq to sell oil for food, for medicine, for other humanitarian supplies for the Iraqi people" [S1] — Iraqi oil already flowed through a capped, UN-supervised channel rather than a free market, consistent with a moderate-disruption reading. What was known December 16: the strikes had begun and targeted military/WMD sites, not oil infrastructure by name [S1]; not known: how many days the campaign would run or how Iraq would respond.
