## Narrative

On 14 April 1995, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 986 by unanimous vote, authorizing states to import Iraqi petroleum and petroleum products in a sum "not exceeding a total of one billion United States dollars every 90 days," with proceeds paid into a UN-administered escrow account to fund humanitarian imports for the Iraqi population [S1]. Funds were also earmarked for a Compensation Fund and for UN inspection and audit costs [S1]. The measure revived a mechanism the Council had first proposed in Resolutions 706 and 712 of 1991, which Iraq had rejected as too limited and an infringement on its sovereignty [S3]. Iraq did not accept Resolution 986 on the day it passed: a memorandum of understanding implementing it was not signed until 20 May 1996, and the first Iraqi oil exports under the program did not begin until 10 December 1996 [S3]. Physically, on 14 April 1995 no additional Iraqi crude reached world markets — the resolution only authorized a future, capped, revenue-limited flow via the Kirkuk-Yumurtalik pipeline and the Mina al-Bakr terminal, subject to a UN sanctions committee's transaction-by-transaction approval [S1]. Whether or when Iraq would use the authorization was unresolved on the day of adoption [S3].

## Not known at the time

Whether Iraq would accept the authorization at all was not known on 14 April 1995: Iraq had rejected a structurally similar 1991 offer (Resolutions 706/712) as an infringement on its sovereignty, and did not sign an implementing memorandum of understanding until 20 May 1996 — thirteen months later — with actual exports beginning only on 10 December 1996 [S3]. The eventual scale, duration, and the program's later expansion (to $5.256 billion per 180-day period by 1998, per a UN press release title found in search but not fetched this session and therefore not cited as a source) were not knowable in April 1995. No source retrieved this session states a contemporaneous (April 1995) market or analyst expectation of the resolution's near-term implementation odds, so this dossier cannot characterize how "surprising" implementation timing was — only that the resolution's own text and the CRS retrospective agree implementation was conditional and did not follow immediately.
