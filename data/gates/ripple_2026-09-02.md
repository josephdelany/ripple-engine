## Addendum, later the same day — Ruling 1 got stronger without anyone touching it

Re-checked `propagation_edges` after other sessions had worked in the tree. The six edges are
now **five validated, one null**. The one that flipped is **Palladium** — the single edge my
registered re-test RETAINED. The five my re-test rejected are all still labelled validated.
So the engine's own gate and my re-test now disagree on all six, in both directions.

Nobody acted on this gate. `src/propagation_graph.py` deletes and rebuilds those rows on every
run, and every strength changed between my two reads (Brent 5.56 → 6.04, Heating oil 5.57 →
5.03, 5Y breakeven 16.74 → 16.25, Palladium 5.22 → 5.14, S&P 2.12 → 1.89, Platinum 7.22 →

on refreshed data, not a decision.

**That is the point.** Palladium's cell now reads `amp 5.1435, ci95 [-0.251, 10.1076],
generalizes false`. The lower bound sits a quarter of a point below zero. A label that turns
over on a routine refresh, at a CI edge of −0.25, is not carrying the weight the word
"validated" implies on a surface.

And the two gates are not measuring the same thing. Theirs (`cross_asset_conditioned.py` line
81): `generalizes = ci_excludes_zero and amp > 0 and survives_FDR`, where `amp` is
**high-VIX events minus low-VIX events**. It never looks at a non-event day. Mine compares the
response against 500 non-event days matched on the same VIX **and** geopolitical-risk state.
The repo already conceded this exact distinction once — `src/placebo_vixmatched.py` opens by
saying the label-shuffle null "rejects under BOTH the real-amplification hypothesis AND the
artifact. It cannot tell them apart" (red-team attack #2, CONCEDED). The event-only median
split has the same defect. So the disagreement between the two gates is structural and
predictable, not bad luck, and the stronger test is the one that says null.

Nothing here changes my recommendation; it removes a reason to hesitate over it. Verify:
